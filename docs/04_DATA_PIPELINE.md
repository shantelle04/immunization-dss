# 04. Data: fully synthetic, seeded, calibrated dataset

Decision D-02 (2026-09-28): the project uses a **fully synthetic dataset**, generated by a seeded simulator whose behaviour is calibrated to published Kenyan figures. No microdata is requested or downloaded. This fits proposal 1.7 ("simulated operational scenarios", "only simulated patient data will be used for development and testing") and removes the DHS access delay from the critical path. Proposal 3.3 names secondary datasets; the author will update that wording (doc 09, P-12).

## 1. Why synthetic, and why it is defensible

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why not real data? | (1) Facility-level weekly stock and child vaccination records sit in national systems (KHIS, LMIS) that are not public; DHS microdata has no stock data and WUENIC is one national figure per year. (2) DHS access needs registration and approval, which the timeline could not absorb. (3) Real child records are sensitive data under the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019, and need ethics approval; proposal 1.7 already commits to simulated patient data. (4) Synthetic data carries ground truth, so the proposal's 95% accuracy target can actually be measured. (5) Scenarios (disruptions, moves, dirty imports) are controllable and reproducible from a seed. The same reasoning, in the author's voice, is section 3 of the progress report |
| Is it realistic? | Behaviour is calibrated so that simulated coverage reproduces the Kenya DHS 2022 Key Indicators Report, Table 11, for all 16 doses plus zero-dose, within 3 percentage points (achieved: within 2.5 in evidence run `sim-seed42-368707d3`; 1.4 before D-18, D-19 and D-23). Population mix uses the same table's weighted counts |
| What does synthetic add? | Ground truth. The simulator knows the true stock-out weeks, the true demand (including children turned away), and every child's true behaviour, so alert accuracy and defaulter accuracy can be measured exactly |
| What is the limitation? | Models are evaluated on data from a process whose rules are designed, not observed. Stated in Chapters 5 and 6, with the calibration evidence |

## 2. Calibration source (published, no request needed)

KNBS and ICF (2023), *Kenya Demographic and Health Survey 2022: Key Indicators Report*, Table 11 "Vaccinations by background characteristics" (public PDF from The DHS Program). Values used, children 12 to 23 months (MR2: 24 to 35 months):

| BCG | OPV0 | OPV1 | OPV2 | OPV3 | Penta1 | Penta2 | Penta3 | PCV1 | PCV2 | PCV3 | Rota1 | Rota2 | IPV | MR1 | MR2 | None |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 96.9 | 86.1 | 96.5 | 94.2 | 78.2 | 97.1 | 93.9 | 89.2 | 96.5 | 95.4 | 91.2 | 96.0 | 92.3 | 87.4 | 89.0 | 66.8 | 2.1 |

Also used: Penta3 by mother's education, wealth quintile and birth order (subgroup effects), and the weighted number of children in each category (population mix). The KEPI schedule (birth, 6, 10, 14 weeks, 9 and 18 months; Rotarix two doses; PCV10; IPV at 14 weeks) is taken from section 3.10 of the same report.

Every other value in `analytics/configs/sim.yaml` is marked ASSUMPTION with a comment, for example vial sizes, session days, lead times, disruption windows. Each is a defendable modelling choice, listed for the panel in `docs/logs/DEFENCE_NOTES.md`.

## 3. How the generator works

```
immdss simulate [--config analytics/configs/sim.yaml] [--seed 42] [--out data/synthetic]
immdss validate-sim data/synthetic/<run_id>
```

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Population | 12 fictional facilities (5 dispensaries, 4 health centres, 3 sub-county hospitals) in "Synthetic County". Births per facility per week (Poisson, 2% annual growth, mild seasonality), from 2 years before the stock window. Each child gets sex, mother's education, wealth quintile, birth order (KDHS proportions), residence, and whether they attend outreach |
| 2. Behaviour | Each child has a risk score from the KDHS subgroup effects. It drives entry into care (zero-dose), returning for each contact (dropout), and lateness (lognormal delays, longer in the rainy months Mar to May and Oct to Dec) |
| 3. Calibration, stage 1 | A 40,000-child cohort without stock limits; intercepts and per-dose "missed opportunity" probabilities are fitted (damped iterations) until coverage matches Table 11 |
| 4. Calibration, stage 2 | The full engine (with stock-outs) is run and parameters are corrected twice, because stock-outs lower coverage |
| 5. Engine | Session by session: children attend fixed or outreach sessions; every dose that is due is offered (catch-up included, KEPI min age, max age, min interval); doses need stock. Weekly: deliveries (monthly cycle, lead time, skipped and partial deliveries, two supply disruptions), open-vial expiry, losses, reordering to a max level, emergency orders. A child turned away by a stock-out returns in 1 to 3 weeks (60%) or at the next contact |
| 6. Dirty imports | Import CSVs with planted defects and an answer key (section 5) |
| 7. Validation | 11 checks (section 6); the run fails if any check fails |

Run time about 1 minute. Same seed and config give byte-identical files (tested).

## 5. Data cleaning in a synthetic project

The supervisor's question "have you started cleaning the datasets" maps to two concrete pieces of work:

1. **Generated data is validated, not assumed** (section 6). Bugs found this way are fixed in the generator, never patched in the output.
2. **The system itself cleans incoming data.** The import files contain planted defects the system's import validation must catch, and the answer key lets the project report cleaning recall and false rejections (proposal 3.2.2, "data ingestion errors"):

| File | Defects planted (about 15% of rows) |
|---|---|
| Immunizations | duplicate row, date before birth, future date, unknown dose code, missing child ID, malformed date, unknown facility |
| Stock | negative receipt, unknown antigen, malformed date, non-numeric quantity |

**Known limitations (doc 07; before and after in `docs/evidence/D-18_D-19_before_after.md` and `docs/evidence/D-23_D-26_before_after.md`):** the no-education group is 5.5 points above KDHS (78.5% vs 73.0%) because the three background factors are drawn independently (D-19). Wastage (WHO definition) is BCG 65.8% (WHO planning figure 50%), MR 46.1% (40%), OPV 6.1%, IPV 4.2%, PCV 1.4%, single-dose about 0.2% (D-18, D-23). Batching BCG and MR to one weekly day caps MR1 coverage at about 86.5% (KDHS 89.0), because 20% of deferred children do not return; the damped second-stage calibration (D-26) settles at 2.4 to 2.9 points and cannot close this gap, which is set by the batching assumption. Batching also raises Penta1 to MR1 dropout at health centres (16.2%) and delays BCG (65.0% within 28 days).

## 7. What the forecasting module sees vs truth

The app only sees issues (doses administered). During stock-outs, administered is lower than true demand (censored demand). `truth/weekly_stock.csv` has both, so the evaluation can report whether forecasts trained on administered doses under-predict after stock-outs. This is a useful finding for Chapter 5 and a question the panel may ask.
